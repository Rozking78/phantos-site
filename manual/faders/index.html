Operator Manual / Faders

6Faders

A fader is a workspace tile. Drop one in the grid, tell it what to drive, and it drives it — hand-tracked, with no crossfade lagging behind your fingers.

How do I add a fader?
  1. Drag down an empty column of the grid. A fader is always one column wide and as tall as you dragged — drag across several columns and you lay down a whole bank in one gesture.
  2. Pick Playback ▸ Fader.
  3. Long-press the LCD panel at the bottom of the fader to choose what it drives.

Anatomy

Top to bottom: a read-out capsule, the throw, and an LCD panel carrying the name and a target badge. The LCD is also a button.

The track is only ever a level

A press anywhere on the throw is a level grab and nothing else — hold it still all day and no menu will appear. The configure long-press lives on the LCD alone (and on the whole face while the fader is unassigned). That is why a fine trim never opens a sheet mid-cue.

Read-outs are in the target's own unit, never a bare percentage: 0–100 for an executor or group, seconds for a time fader (or the word cue when nothing overrides the cue), m:ss / m:ss for a scrub, frames for a transition rate.

Time faders use a square-law throw — the bottom half of the travel is the first quarter of the range — so short times, where you actually work, get most of the fader. The top defaults to 10 seconds; change it in the config sheet or with max.

Looping a scrub fader

A scrub fader's LCD area is a transport bar: play / pause, stop and ↻ loop. Tap ↻ to loop the slot. You do not have to open anything — loop sits under the same thumb that works the throw, and you can arm it before or during playback.

  • Lit ↻ means the slot is looping on the wire, or is armed to loop the next time you play it. The lamp reads the media node's own answer, so a loop armed from the Media window, from another console or with media 203 loop lights this key too.
  • Arming while it plays retunes it immediately — the clip you are already watching starts looping, it does not wait for the end.
  • Pause ends the loop mode; the arm survives it. A paused slot has no transport mode running, but the key stays lit because your next play will start it looping again.
  • Scrubbing never disarms loop. Drag the throw anywhere, as often as you like — a seek changes where, never how. The same holds for bounce and reverse.

Tapping a lit ↻ turns the loop off, including a loop somebody else armed. Off is play-without-loop, not a stop — the clip keeps rolling and simply ends when it ends.

Putting an attribute on a fader

Pick Attribute as the target and the sheet asks two questions. First what it drives: a fixture or a group, then its number. Then the tree — a row of feature groups (Intensity, Position, Colour, Beam, and whatever else the fixture actually has), and under the one you tap, its attributes. Tap one and the fader is that channel.

The tree is the Programmer panel's own list, so a fader can never offer you a channel the panel does not. It shows only throw channels: gobo wheels, colour wheels, toggles and one-shot buttons are real, but they are not faders, and they stay on the Programmer panel where they are drawn as the control they are.

An attribute fader drives its own target, never your selection

Grabbing it does not select anything and does not change what you have selected. It writes straight to the fixture — or to every member of the group — that the LCD names. The throw is a hand: it lands instantly and cancels any fade running on that channel, so the light is always where your finger is. The LCD button is the one thing that touches the selection, and only because you pressed it: it puts you on the fixture, or recalls the group.

A group of mixed fixture types is fine. The menu is built from the first member's profile, the write is re-resolved on each member's own, and members that have no such channel are left alone — the fader says so on its face: Iris (4/6). It never rounds that up to “fine”.

The read-out is the channel's own DMX value — 0–255, or 0–65535 on a 16-bit channel — the same number the Programmer panel's slider shows for the same attribute. The cap picks up from what is already there: your programmer first, then what another console is holding, then the channel's default. It never snaps.

Configuring one

Long-press the LCD. The sheet gives you the target, the number (it asks — a fresh fader is never silently pointed at number 1), a name, twelve colours or “by type”, and whatever else that target needs: the button action, the top of a time range, loop for a scrub, the channel for a T-bar. Every assigned fader also gets a RESET row: a master gets a Reset to neutral button, and a fader that is not a master gets a line saying so and naming what does release it. Unassign asks twice. The sheet also prints its own command-line address, so you can script what you just built by hand.

Taking the tile off. The sheet's Remove tile button (it asks twice too) clears the fader off the workspace entirely. It is there because a fader is the one tile you cannot swipe over: a drag that starts on a fader belongs to the fader, so the marquee that re-assigns every other tile never begins on one. Unassign empties the target and leaves the empty fader standing; Remove tile takes the tile.

From the command line

Faders are addressed by their grid cell, counting from 1 — r1 c1 is the top-left cell.

fader                          list every fader tile, its target and its value
fader 2 1 exec 3               row 2, column 1 drives executor 3
fader 2 1 name Chorus          label it (blank restores the entity's own name)
fader 2 1 color amber          tint it
fader 2 1 button go+           what its LCD button does
fader 2 1 max 10               top of the range, 0.1-600s (time faders)
fader 2 1 loop on              loop (scrub faders) - same as the tile's loop key
fader 2 1 attribute 12         drive an attribute on fixture 12
fader 2 1 attribute group 3    …or on every member of group 3
fader 2 1 scope group 4        re-point an attribute fader at group 4
fader 2 1 attr <name>          which channel (attribute / T-bar / transition faders)
fader 2 1 prog on|off          group fader: Prog Mode (on = programmer, off = master)
fader 2 1 reset                put a master fader back to neutral
fader 2 1 clear                unassign it (the empty tile stays)
fader 2 1 remove               take the tile off the workspace

On an attribute fader, attr with a name it cannot find prints the tree — every feature group and its throw channels — and leaves the fader on the channel it already had. It never parks a tile on a name that does not exist.

A bad address lists the real ones rather than failing quietly. Note that fader configures the tile — to move a level, use exec 1 master 50 or group 4 @ 50 (the programmer — what a Prog Mode fader writes). The group master — what the fader writes by default — has no typed form; it lives on the fader itself and on the group edit sheet.

A group fader drives its group

Not “whatever happens to be selected”. By default it writes its group's master and never touches the selection at all. In Prog Mode grabbing it makes its group the selection, and if the selection drifts under you — a second fader, a peer console, a stuck grab — a fader labelled G4 re-asserts group 4. That is deliberate.

When a fader refuses

An assigned fader that cannot do its job replaces the throw with a warning triangle and the reason, and writes nothing. You will see it for an empty group, an empty or missing media slot, a clip whose duration is not known yet, no ATEM patched, or a profile with no T-bar channel. An attribute fader refuses for an unpatched fixture, an empty group, no attribute chosen yet, a profile that has no such channel, or a channel that is a dropdown or a button rather than a throw — and it names which. A refusing fader is telling you about the rig, not about itself. It also parks at zero and writes nothing: an attribute fader with nothing to drive never falls back to driving everything.

The grand master

Bottom right of the screen, on the bezel itself: a real fader with the level beside it. It is grab-relative — it moves from wherever you take hold, never jumping to the finger. The Grand Master window carries the same value again; so does gm 80 from the command line, with blackout and flash.

The grand master is the master of the stage, not of the programmer. It is the last word on intensity: it comes down on everything that is lighting the room — your programmer and every running executor, on this console and on every other console in the session. Blackout and Flash are the same control at its ends, so they take the executors with them too. It dims only intensity; a gobo, a colour wheel or a position never re-selects because you moved a master.

That window carries three pages, picked from the tiles across the top:

  • Master — the grand master fader, the lock, and the momentary Flash and BO.
  • Program — five quick channel faders (Int, Pan, Tilt, Zoom, Focus), the colour swatches, and Clear. They drive whatever is selected, or everything when nothing is.
  • Rate / Width — the effect rate and width masters, live against running effects, with TAP beating a tempo into Rate 1.
