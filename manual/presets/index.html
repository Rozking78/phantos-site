5Presets & executors

Presets are looks by type. Executors are cue lists that play them back.

Recording a preset

How do I save the look I have in the programmer?
  1. Build it. Select, set values.
  2. Type record preset on its own — the type menu opens — or name the type directly: record preset colour lands in the next free colour slot.
  3. Or aim at a slot: record preset 5.1. The pool that slot belongs to decides the type.
  4. label preset 5.1 Warm Wash names it.

Presets are numbered pool.slot: the whole number is the pool, the decimal is the slot inside it. Each pool holds nine slots, 1 through 9, and a new record takes the lowest free one, so gaps get reused. If you give both a slot and a type word, they have to agree; the console refuses rather than guess, and tells you where it would have filed the preset.

Write the decimal

preset 3 is not pool 3 — it is the raw number 3, which shows on a tile as 0.3. You almost always want the decimal form: preset 3.1.

Recording onto an occupied slot is refused on purpose. Use update preset 5.1 to fold what you are holding into it, or merge preset 5.1 — they are the same merge. Tapping an occupied tile while Record is armed asks whether you meant Merge or Overwrite.

Preset types are Intensity, Position, Colour, Gobo, Beam, Effects, Shaper, Video, Time, Control, Layouts and Timecode. A typed record captures only that type — a colour preset stores colour, and refuses rather than quietly saving a whole look. record preset all takes everything.

Every one of those types can also be combined — Presets ▸ Combos lists each feature, and under it the pairs it makes. Control combines like the rest of them; its combination pools are numbered from 1000 up, stacked above the single and combination pools so that adding it moved nothing that was already numbered.

Recalling

  • preset 3.1 or recall preset 3.1, or tap the tile in a preset pool.
  • preset 3.1 out — release what that preset put in.
  • preset 3.1 mask ic — take only some attribute families from it (intensity and colour here). Letters are the type initials.
  • Recall obeys the fade switch, so a preset crossfades in over the Prog Fade time.

A preset asserts above playback, and the console remembers which preset owns each attribute — that is what puts the green ring on a pool tile.

Where a preset lands: the three recall modes

ModeWhat it does
Selective (default)Uses the values as recorded. If you recall it onto a different number of fixtures, the console lays the recorded values out as a line across the new selection and samples along it — so a five-lamp colour spread stretches sensibly onto eight lamps. Continuous values blend; gobo slots and other switched values snap to the nearest recorded one, because halfway between two gobos is a different gobo.
FixtureApplies to fixtures of the same profile — your selection if you have one, otherwise every fixture of that profile.
WorldApplies across profiles by attribute name — your selection if you have one, otherwise the whole rig. Fixtures without that attribute are skipped.

Set it with preset 3.1 mode selective|fixture|world, or tap the S / F / W badge in the corner of the pool tile to cycle it. To pick a mode outright rather than cycling, open the preset's edit sheet — the three modes are the capsule row at the top of it.

That sheet is where a preset is changed: recall mode, update (merge the programmer in), overwrite, rename or delete. Every pool tile opens its sheet the same two ways — right-click it, or hold it for half a second (the one exception is a Flash executor, whose hold is the flash itself). Each is one gesture and one stage: nothing pops up in between asking which command you meant. Arming Edit and tapping is the third way in, and all three land on the same sheet. This is true of every preset tile, in a preset zone on the main screen and in the Preset Pool window alike — and of group and executor tiles too.

With nothing selected, a preset re-selects the group it was recorded from, if it had one — so recalling a preset can bring its fixtures back with it.

An empty slot in the Preset Pool is a live target: tapping it arms a record there. A pool tile shows only presets whose type matches the pool exactly, so a colour-and-position preset lives in the colour+position pool, not in the colour pool.

The Preset Pool window itself reads top to bottom: the pool and its tiles, the feature filters (All, then one chip per type), Prog Time for the recall fade, the grand master with Flash and Blackout, and the quick-programmer channel faders. Pool… at the top right opens the drill-down that picks which pool you are looking at — All, All-in-One, one type, or a combination, and the capsule beside it names the pool you are in — Pool 24 over tiles that read 24.0 to 24.9.

Prog Time here is the same control as the Prog Fade bezel, so it carries the same Link / Unlink: linked is one time, unlink splits it and the Fade Out fader seeds itself from Fade In. The card's capsule reads 2s linked, 2s/5s split, and snap with Prog Time off — the same words the bezel button wears.

Executors and cues

How do I build a cue list?
  1. Build your first look in the programmer.
  2. record exec 1 — the look becomes executor 1, cue 1.
  3. Build the next look, then record exec 1 cue to add it as the next cue.
  4. Open Cue Lists to see, name, retime and reorder them.
  5. go 1 runs it. back 1 steps back. release 1 lets it go.
  • update exec 1 cue 2 folds what you are holding into that cue. merge exec 1 cue 2 adds to it while keeping what it already had; add refs to fold in live preset references rather than baked values.
  • goto cue 3 exec 2 jumps without walking the list. Name the executor — with no exec, goto goes to executor 1.
  • exec 1 cue 2 move up|down|<position> reorders. The cue numbers stay where they are — a 1 · 5 · 10 list is still 1 · 5 · 10 after a move — and if you move the cue that is playing, playback follows it.
  • label exec 1 cue 2 Chorus names a cue.

How a cue list holds a look

Cues are deltas, and the console tracks. Running a cue replays every cue from the last block cue forward, so a cue only has to hold what changes. That is why recording just what is in your programmer is the right thing to do, and why jumping backwards correctly removes the changes the later cues made. Mark a cue as Block to make it a hard starting point that inherits nothing.

Cues that reference presets

A cue can hold a preset by reference instead of by value. exec 1 cue 2 ref 5 …, or the preset chip's own menu, chooses which:

ModeBehaviour
Referenced (default)The cue follows the preset. Change the preset and every cue referencing it changes.
LockedThe values are frozen at today's version, but the cue still remembers which preset it came from. Later preset edits do not reach it.
AbsoluteThe values are baked in and the reference is thrown away. There is no going back to referenced from here.

A value you typed into the cue by hand always beats the referenced preset on that same attribute.

Executor options

Set these in Cue Lists, or from the command line. All of them take an argument — see the warning below.

OptionEffect
exec 1 priority <0-200>Where this executor sits when two executors want the same channel. Executors default to 50; your programmer sits above them at 110.
exec 1 master <0-100>The executor's own level. Moving it is instant — a master never restarts a crossfade.
exec 1 loop on|offRuns the list round. Without it, GO stops on the last cue instead of wrapping.
exec 1 momentary on|offRuns only while held. Shows as Flash in Cue Lists.
exec 1 parked on|offImmune to Release All and to master release. Use it for house light and anything that must not go out.
exec 1 touch toggle|go+|go-|flashWhat a press on its tile or fader button does. Typing go 1 always means GO regardless.
exec 1 fade 2 out 4This executor's own times. exec 1 fade reads them back; exec 1 fade clear removes them. They only supersede the cue with overridetime on.
exec 1 overridetime on|offOff by default: the executor runs on its cue times. On: its own fade time supersedes every cue time. exec 1 overridetime reads it back. The Exec Fade bezel still wins over both.
exec 1 timing cueIgnore the Exec Fade bezel and always use the cue's stored times. timing master puts it back under the bezel.

Finish the sentence

An exec line the console does not recognise falls through to a GO. Typing exec 1 loop without on or off will fire executor 1 rather than refuse. Always give the argument.

Releasing is not fading to zero

Releasing an executor re-resolves the stack and reveals what is underneath. Only channels nothing else is holding actually ramp out. That is what makes busking work — release the top look and the one below it is simply there.

The Cue Lists window

Executors are tiles down the left, in the same colours they wear on the main screen. Tap one and its cue list fills the right: one big row per cue carrying the number, the name and — as chips — Fade In, Fade Out, Wait In, Wait Out, Dwell, Follow, Block, Priority and what the cue fires on GO. Tap a row and it opens into its editor: every value is a cell that raises the keypad, with a − and a + beside it, and the cue's own Up · Down · Merge · Replace · Delete sit along the bottom of it. Transport is Back · Go · Release, with the executor's properties beneath them. The playing cue's row is green. Times clamp at 600 seconds; a cue number already in use is refused. The property toggles under the transport include Exec Time > Cue — off by default, so the executor runs on its cue times; turn it on and this executor's own fade time supersedes every cue time.

A cue with a Dwell runs on by itself after that long; a cue marked Follow runs on as soon as its wait and fade are done. Any manual GO cancels a pending follow. Auto-follows always use the cue's own times, whatever the Exec Fade bezel says.

Effects

Open FX to build one: pick the attribute, the waveform, the rate, the size and the spread across the selection. fx fire starts it, fx stop stops it, fx rev reverses.

Two buttons save an effect, and they do different things:

  • ● Pool stores the effect's shape only, in the FX window's own pool. Recalling it points that shape at whatever you have selected now, without restarting the wave.
  • ● Preset stores it as an ordinary preset, listed in the FX Presets window; tap to re-fire.

Multi-axis effects want ● Pool

● Preset keeps only the first axis, so a circle recorded that way comes back as a single sweep. Use ● Pool for anything with more than one axis running.

An FX pool tile is a tap to recall. To throw one away, hold it (or right-click it) and take Delete.

And remember: a Clear does not stop effects.
