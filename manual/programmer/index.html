Operator Manual / Programmer & Clear

3Programmer & Clear

The programmer is what you are holding. It sits on top of playback, it is yours alone, and it stays there until you give it back.

Putting values in

  • 1 thru 8 @ 50 — select and set intensity in one line.
  • at +10 / at -10 — nudge. Each fixture moves from its own level, so a spread stays a spread.
  • color red, cct 5400, pan 60, zoom 40, gobo circle — attributes by name.
  • hue red thru blue — a line of colour laid across the selection in fan order.

No selection, no write

Every line above is selection-scoped: it lands on the fixtures you are holding. With an empty selection it is a no-op — nothing is written, nothing is recorded, and the status line reads “no selection”. This is deliberate: a bare full typed out of habit, or tapped on the deck between looks, must never take the whole rig to 100%. The verbs that do mean everything — Master Clear, Release All, unpatch all — say so in their own name and never rely on an empty selection to get there.

Clear: values first, then the selection

The Clear law

Each press clears the next non-empty stage: VALUES first (red), the SELECTION second (green). Your values release on the first press — over the Prog Fade time when fade is armed — and your hand keeps its fixtures until the second press lets them go. An empty stage is skipped, so with nothing selected one press still drops the values, and with nothing at all Clear quietly does nothing.

The status line names the stage: “Cleared values — selection held; press again to deselect”, then “Cleared selection”.

Two things that surprise people

  • The stage comes from what exists, not from a counter. Put new values in between presses and the next Clear drops those values again; the selection goes on the press after. Session traffic from other consoles and media playhead ticks can never rob you of a stage.
  • Pressing Clear during a clear fade snaps it complete. The ramp jumps to done rather than restarting the ladder.

What Clear does not touch

  • Executors keep running. Clear is the programmer; Release All is playback.
  • Other operators' values. Clear only drops rows this console authored — which is why the button lights red only for what you can actually give back.
  • Group masters. See Groups & masters. This is the point of them.
  • Grand master, blackout, flash, patch, layouts, fade times.

Running effects survive a Clear

A Clear does not stop an effect. Stop it from the FX window, or with fx stop, before you wonder why a light is still moving.

Clearing one kind of thing

The per-domain buttons — Clear Light, Clear Cam, Clear Switch, Clear Audio, Clear Media, Clear Display — are the same two-press ladder, narrowed to one kind of fixture. Press 1 drops just that domain's fixtures out of your selection; press 2 releases that domain's values.

Domain press 2 crosses the session

A plain Clear is yours alone. A per-domain press 2 is a master clear of that domain: every console in the session releases its own values for those fixtures. Arming one domain also disarms any other domain you had primed, so Clear Light then Clear Cam can never hand Cam a loaded second press.

Fade — one dial, one switch

The fade law

The switch decides, the dial remembers. With Fade off, everything snaps — whatever seconds are dialled in. With Fade on, the dialled time applies. Turning the switch off does not lose your time.

Open Prog Fade — the same panel opens under Prog Time, and the executor pair under Exec Fade and Exec Time. You get:

  • Fade — the green arm switch. This is the one that decides.
  • Link / Unlink — linked means Fade Out follows Fade In. Unlink to split them; the Out fader seeds itself from the In time so you never start from zero. Link is only reachable while Fade is on.
  • Fade In and Fade Out — two vertical faders, 0 to 10 seconds. Grab anywhere on the fader and drag: the time moves with your finger from where it already was, so touching the fader never jumps the time. Hold Shift while dragging for fine control.

A value moving up uses Fade In; a value moving down uses Fade Out. A bezel button carrying Prog Fade shows the time on its face — 2s when linked, 2s/4s when split — and goes green while the fade is armed.

From the command line, time 3 (or fade 3) sets three seconds and arms the switch; time 0 disarms it. You can also hang a time on the end of any command: preset 4 time 2.

Why some things still snap with fade armed

  • Hand-tracked moves. A fader you are dragging, a dial you are turning and a group fader follow your hand exactly. A typed destination fades; a live gesture tracks.
  • Switched channels. Dropdowns, toggles and wheel slots land instantly — halfway between two gobos is not a value, and a shape list never sweeps through the twenty shapes in between on its way from one to another.
  • Move-in-black channels. Profile channels marked as snap (ATEM sources, some wheels) hold their old value until the fixture is dark or the fade has run, then jump.
  • Fixtures with a real mechanical time use the fixture's own time, not yours.

When a fan is armed at the same time as a fade, the fixtures start in fan order rather than together — the spread arrives as a wave.

Effects morph out; they do not pop. Fade a look away and the continuous half of a prism, prismatic or animation closes first — spread, index and spin travel back to their rest positions — while the pick itself (the prism type, the facet count, the pattern) stays on its slot. The pick drops only once those parameters have landed, so the effect dissolves instead of disappearing at full spread. Fading a look in is the other way round: the pick lands first and the parameters open under it.

Executor fade

Exec Fade is the same panel for playback. While it is armed it overrides the times stored in your cues, and the panel says so at the bottom: overrides cue times or honoring cue times. It applies to manual GOs and releases only — an auto-follow or a loop always uses the cue's own times, and an executor set to locked cue timing ignores the bezel entirely.

An executor's own fade time does not touch its cues until you say so. Turn on Exec Time > Cue (the toggle in Cue Lists, or exec <n> overridetime on) and this executor's stored fade time supersedes every one of its cue times, GO and release alike. Leave it off — the default — and the executor honors the times written in its cues, even if a fade time is stored on it. The armed Exec Fade bezel still overrides everything above that.

Order of precedence for a manual GO: armed Exec Fade → the executor's own fade time (only when Exec Time > Cue is on) → the cue's time.

Undo

  • Fifty steps of history. Recording something new discards the redo pile.
  • Undo and redo honour the fade switch: off snaps, on crossfades over the Prog Fade time.
  • One fader gesture is one step, not two hundred.
  • Undoing a recorded pool change puts the old look back instantly, on purpose.

What undo will not bring back

Selection is not undoable — undo restores values, not what you had highlighted. Nor are align mode, highlight, grand master, patch, layouts or fade times. And while you are in a session, executor go/release is not undoable: playback undo works on a standalone console only. Release and re-fire instead.
