Operator Manual / The screen

2The screen

One window, four bands: a top bezel, the workspace, a bottom bezel, and the command line. Panels slide over the workspace; they never rearrange it.

Top bezel

  • Broker lamp — green with the word Broker when this console is in a session, red and offline when it is not.
  • Fixtures and Prog — two fixed tabs that slide the fixture list and the programmer panel in over the workspace from the edges. Tap again to slide them away. Where each one opens is yours — see Where the panels open.
  • Bezel buttons 0–11 — the top row of assignable buttons.

Workspace

The middle band. Tiles fill it; the Fixtures and Programmer panels slide over it from the edges rather than squeezing it; a bezel window pops up on top. The Fixtures panel is a wall of fixture tiles — tap to select, long-press to edit. The Prog panel is built from the profiles of whatever you have selected, so it shows that fixture's real channels and nothing else.

Where the panels open

Fixtures and Programmer come up in the same place at the same size every time. Out of the box Fixtures is the left vertical quarter and Programmer the right vertical quarter — the widths the console has always used. Either can be moved to any of seven places:

  • Left ¼ and Right ¼ — the vertical quarter panes.
  • Left ½ and Right ½ — vertical half-screen panes: exactly half the workspace, full height.
  • Top ⅓ and Bottom ⅓ — a full-width band across the workspace.
  • Full Screen — the panel fills the workspace.

The Programmer's faders follow the shape of the pane. In a quarter pane — tall and narrow — the attributes stack as full-width rows, exactly as they always have. In a Top ⅓ or Bottom ⅓ band — and on Full Screen, which is a wide surface too — the pane is wider than it is tall, so the faders stand up: one column per attribute, side by side, each with its name above and its level, −, ⌂ and + below, and the feature-group buttons and the Align strip on a single line above them. Drag a fader up and down; hold ⇧ while you drag for a fine one. Nothing about the quarter or half panes changes — both are taller than they are wide, so their faders stay as stacked rows.

Change the default in Settings ▸ Screen ▸ Panes: one row per panel, tap it and pick the place. Your choice is kept on this console and comes back next launch. The same seven places are also bezel options — long-press a bezel slot, open the Panes tab and assign e.g. Programmer Full Screen. Pressing that key puts the panel there and opens it; pressing it again while the panel is already there closes it, and the place is remembered either way.

Bottom bezel

  • Bezel buttons 12–29 — the bottom row. Thirty assignable slots in total.
  • Grand master — the fader at the far right, with the level beside it. Drag it. It is grab-relative: it moves from wherever you take hold, so a mistap on the busiest strip on the console cannot snap the show to zero. For Flash, BO and the lock open the Grand Master window; from the command line, gm 80, blackout, flash.

A fresh console starts with the first seven top-row buttons filled: Settings, Save Show, Load Show, Patch, Undo, Redo, Clear. Everything else is blank and waiting.

Assigning a bezel button

How do I put a function on a bezel button?
  1. Press and hold the button for half a second. The assign sheet opens.
  2. Pick a tab: Windows (surfaces that open), Actions (things that fire once), Toggles (panels that slide).
  3. Tap the function. The sheet closes and the button takes it.
  4. Turn on Lock first if you are assigning several buttons in a row — the sheet stays open.
  5. Clear slot empties the button again.

An empty slot shows a faint ＋ Assign. Tapping an empty slot does nothing on purpose — assignment is long-press only, so a fat finger during a show cannot pop a sheet over your work.

Once assigned: tap = do it, long-press = reassign. A button whose function has no backend yet is outlined in orange rather than the teal affordance colour, and says so in the log when you press it. Nothing in this console pretends to work.

How do I take a function off a button?
  1. Tap Delete to arm it.
  2. Tap the bezel button. It is unbound, back to ＋ Assign.
  3. It does not fire the function on the way out, and it does not touch what the button pointed at — deleting a Save Show button does not touch the show.
  4. Delete disarms itself after one deletion. Tapping an already-blank slot leaves it armed, so you can go and hit the one you meant.

The workspace grid

The middle of the screen is a grid of cells you fill with tiles. Drag across empty cells to choose a block, and the tile picker opens; pick a category, then an item.

CategoryWhat you can drop
PresetsPreset pools, by type — All, All-in-One, one type, or a combination.
FixturesAll Fixtures, Cameras, Media, DMX Fixtures.
PlaybackExecutors, Groups, Fader.
ContentVideo Viewer, Time Display, Universe Monitor, System, Canvas, Multi-View, Sessions, Command Keypad, Manual.
ActionsClear, Release All, Record, Undo, Redo, Delete, New Show, Save Show, Load Show and the per-domain clears and releases — the bezel functions, as tiles.

Clear is the red button at the top of the picker, beside Cancel. It empties the selected cells again. It sits outside the category tabs on purpose — it is an action, not a category — so it is one tap away whichever tab you are on, and it is the only route: there is no “Clear Tile” item hidden in the Content tab any more.

A tile keeps its saved cells. If the console window is shorter than it was when you laid the grid — a layout made full-screen, opened windowed — the bottom row is cut at the grid’s edge, above the command line, and a pool tile there scrolls to reach the rest. Nothing is painted under the command line or the bottom bezel.

Tiles do not drag around

There is no drag-to-move and no resize handle. To change a tile: use the small grid glyph in its top-right corner to re-assign it in place, or start a fresh drag from an empty cell across the area you want — laying a new tile removes whatever it overlaps.

A preset pool tile fills its block with the pool's tiles. The first tile names the pool — long-press it to set the number range it shows, or tap it while Record is armed to record into that pool's type.

How do I save the layout I just built?
  1. Tap Record (a bezel button or an Actions tile). It arms — it does not pop a chooser.
  2. Tap an empty bezel slot. The workspace is captured onto that slot as a recall layout, with a screenshot of itself as the button face.
  3. Tapping that button later restores the layout. Tapping it while Record is armed overwrites it.

A layout holds the open windows, the panel toggles and every tile in the grid. It deliberately does not hold your bezel buttons — recalling a layout never rewrites the button row out from under you. Arm Edit and tap a layout button to rename it or delete it.

Use a bezel slot, not the generic action

The Recall Layout action assigned from a picker has no layout attached and will simply say so. Layouts are recorded onto a bezel slot with Record, as above.

Editing a pool tile — right-click it, hold it, or arm Edit and tap

Every pool tile is edited the same way, by three routes that all land in one step: right-click the tile, hold the tile for half a second, or arm Edit on a bezel button or an Actions tile and tap it. What opens is sized for a finger. Nothing is hidden behind a right-click, and nothing pops up in between to ask which command you meant.

Right-click, hold, or tap with Edit armedWhat opens
A preset tileRecall mode (Selective / Fixture / World), Update (merge the programmer in), Overwrite (replace), Rename, Delete.
A group tileMasters — mode, Level, Priority — plus Recall / Add sel. / Toggle, Overwrite from selection, Merge selection in, Grid, Z-Order, Rename and Delete.
An executor tileThe Cue Lists window, focused on that executor. One exception: an executor set to Flash (or Momentary) is active-while-held, so holding it flashes it — a flash is never interrupted by a window. Right-click it, or arm Edit and tap.
A layout bezel buttonRename or delete the layout.

Arm Delete or Copy instead and the tap acts at once — no sheet, no confirmation, but every one of them is undoable. Arm Record and tap a tile that already holds something and the console asks first: a preset offers Merge or Overwrite, an executor offers Add Cue, Merge into that cue or Replace it. Merge and Overwrite refuse an empty programmer.

Rename opens the on-screen keyboard with the caret at the end of the current name, so the first key you press adds to the name instead of wiping it. A hardware keyboard types straight into the same field.

Z-Order — stagger depth across a group

From the group sheet, Z-Order takes one value, 0 to 255, and staggers the Z (depth) attribute across the group's members in the programmer — live, and recordable like anything else you set by hand. A value of 128 or more stacks downward from it; below 128 it stacks upward. The step shrinks automatically so a big group still fits inside 0–255. Fixtures with no Z or depth attribute are skipped, and the status line tells you how many were hit.

The console's Align mode decides the shape: normally the offset grows one position at a time down the group, but with Fold << or Fold >> armed it mirrors about the group's centre — the outer pair furthest apart, the ends matching. The sheet shows which shape is in force so you are never guessing.

Moving a group in the pool — both directions

The Move key has two armed states. Press it once and it reads ▲ earlier: tap a group tile and it moves one slot earlier in the pool. Press it again and it reads ▼ later: the next tap moves the group one slot later. A third press turns it off. The group's edit sheet carries the same pair as buttons — Move earlier and Move later — and the command line still takes group 4 move up / group 4 move down. A move swaps the group's number with its neighbour's, so the sheet closes and the pool re-sequences in front of you.

Operator Focus is on by default; switch it off on the single row under Multi-Window in Settings ▸ Screen, beside the picker that places every other detached window, and it will say so there rather than disappearing.

A window that fills the workspace has a red Close and an orange Lock at the top. Lock pins it open: neither Close nor a tap outside will dismiss it. Smaller windows float over the tiles and a tap outside closes them, unless they are locked. Multi-View puts that same Close and Lock at the head of its own control row, just left of Merge, instead of on a strip of their own — one row less between you and the pictures. On a narrow window the shape and A–G keys drop to a second line so nothing is cut off.

Second and third displays

How do I put a surface on another monitor?
  1. Open Settings ▸ Screen.
  2. Choose what to show: Full Console, Fixtures, Programmer, Multi-View, Universe Monitor, Timecode (GPW), Video Viewer, Operator Focus, Manual, Warp / Blend, Gobos or Media.
  3. Turn Full screen on first if you want the window to fill that display every time it opens.
  4. Tap the display you want it on. The window opens there and is remembered.

Windows are pinned to the physical panel, not to a screen number, so they come back on the right monitor after a reboot or a hot-plug. If a pinned monitor is missing, the window stays closed and reappears when the panel returns — it will never jump onto a screen that is feeding an output. Closing a window with its red button does not delete it; reopen it from the same Settings row. Every detached window can go full screen — ⌃⌘F or the green button, Esc to come back — and the Full screen button on its Settings row makes that its habit, so it opens filling the display from then on.

Warp / Blend, Gobos and Media are on that list too: put the warp editor on the display it is warping and you can see the canvas and the wall at once, and either library gets a monitor's width instead of a panel over the tiles. A detached window is the SAME window as the one the bezel tile opens — one warp, one gobo library, one media pool, shown in two places, so an edit on the second display is live on the first.

The command line

Left to right: the terminal icon, the entry field, ? (this manual), the microphone, the comms indicator when a listening channel is armed, history up/down chevrons, and the status line.

  • Tab completes the first word from the verb list.
  • The chevrons walk your command history; they grey out when there is none.
  • The command line, the Command Keypad window and the keypad tile are three windows onto one line of text. Typing on the keypad fills the bar.

The Command Keypad

A touch keypad for the command line — as a bezel window, or as a workspace tile that needs at least two columns by three rows to draw keys big enough to hit. It has two pages; the digits and operators never move between them, so muscle memory holds. The keypad never scrolls and is never cut off: the window is sized to its keys, and on a small screen the keys shrink to the touch floor rather than losing the Enter row.

Two clears, and they are not the same

C← (orange) wipes the line you are typing. It never touches the programmer. CLEAR (red) sends the clear command — the two-press ladder. Tap it twice to give the values back.
