7Media & time

Media is patched like any other fixture and driven like any other fixture. The difference is that clips have a duration, and the desk shows you how much of it is left.

Slots and transport

Media slots run 201 to 255. Slots 191–200 are a read-only capture strip the render node owns. Open Media to see them: tap an occupied tile to select it and point your selection at it; tap an empty tile just to select the slot. Press and hold a tile — or right-click it — for Assign, Play/Pause, Stop, Loop, Clear and Copy CLI, which puts the exact command on your clipboard. The same press-and-hold opens the actions on a library tile, and in the Gobos window on a slot or a piece of art (there Assign, Unassign, Retire from library, Organize and Copy CLI). Every action list is reachable by finger; nothing needs a mouse button, and the list is flat — where an action has choices under it, such as Assign to, its slots are listed straight underneath rather than behind a second menu. One gesture, one layer.

Organize opens the slot grid. Press and hold a slot there — or right-click it — to Unassign it, to see what presets and executors still point at it, or to copy its CLI. Holding a slot that is live on air still lists Unassign, and still refuses it, saying so on the row. Drag a slot to move it; a press that moves is a drag, so only a press that stays put opens the list. An empty slot opens nothing, because there is nothing to do to it.

media 3 assign video <source>   put a clip in slot 3
media 3 play                     roll it
media 3 pause                    hold it
media 3 stop                     stop it
media 3 loop                     loop it
media 3 seek 40                  jump to 40 seconds
media 3 clear                    empty the slot
media rescan                     re-read the library on every node
media window                     open the Media window

You can use the slot's name instead of its number: media Chorus play. If the name is ambiguous the console lists the candidates rather than guessing. A number always wins over a name.

media <n> clear refuses while that slot is live on the canvas, in the same words the render node would use. media rescan asks every node and waits ten seconds; you get a green summary if they all answer, amber if some did, red if none did — it never claims a scan that did not happen.

Uploading: ＋ New ▸ Upload files in the Media window, up to 64 MB per file. ＋ New opens a pop-out list you tap, in Media and in Gobos alike — the same list carries Rescan.

Content slots — gobos, stills, generated images — are the content verb. content 30 on its own points the current selection at slot 30, the same write as gobo 30. content list, content info 30, content 30 assign <asset>, content move 30 40, content swap 30 40 and content insert 30 40 manage the library — and all of them, except assign, refuse while a slot is live.

Time displays

Put a Time Display tile in the workspace. Each one shows one thing:

KindShows
ClockTime of day.
SolarTime to the next solar event, tinted by the phase — indigo at night through amber in the golden hour.
Video elapsedHow far into the clip you are.
Video remainingTime to run.
Video durationThe clip's length.
Video countdownTime to run, counting down.

Tap the sliders button in the top-right corner of the label tile — or long-press the label tile anywhere — to configure it: what it shows, which slot it follows, whether it prints milliseconds, and whether it carries end-jump tiles. Both open the same sheet. A tile holds its last good reading rather than blanking, and shows a small orange dash when it has nothing.

Which clip is “Top Video”

Leave the target on Top Video and the display follows the playing slot with the lowest number, preferring one with a real known duration. That is deliberate: a still reports itself as playing with no duration, so without the preference a still would win and the countdown would read zero. Point a display at a specific slot instead if you want it pinned.

The countdown colours

Time to runColour
more than 15 snormal
15 s or lessyellow
10 s or lessred

The colour never carries the warning alone: while it is on, the label tile swaps its clock for a warning triangle and prints ENDING, so the state reads on a washed-out FOH monitor and to an operator who cannot separate the two hues.

They only appear on a real countdown: a remaining or countdown display, following a slot that is actually playing, on a clip at least fifteen seconds long, with time still to run. A still, a parked slot, or a clip shorter than the warning window stays neutral — a tile that is always red tells you nothing.

End-jump tiles

A video time display can carry three jump buttons — −30, −15 and −10, each labelled GO TO. One tap seeks that clip to that many seconds before its end. It is the “get to the button” control: when a song is called early, tap −15 and the video is at its last fifteen seconds.

How do I turn the end-jump tiles on?
  1. Open the time display's config: tap the sliders button on its label tile, or long-press the tile.
  2. Set the display to a video kind if it is not already.
  3. Switch End-jump tiles ON.
  • A tap seeks only. It does not start playback — a paused clip simply relocates.
  • A tile whose jump would land before the start of the clip is greyed out and does not fire. On a twelve-second clip, −30 and −15 are dead and −10 seeks to two seconds.
  • They act on the clip the display is following.

Video on screen

  • Video Viewer tile — one live source in a workspace cell. Each tile remembers its own source across relaunches. Opened from the bezel it fills the screen and carries the PTZ pad.
  • Multi-View — up to sixteen sources at once, in eight shapes, with merge and A–G layout slots. Opened on a display it goes full screen; Esc comes back.
  • Canvas Preview — the output canvas, at three sizes.
  • DOOM is off in this beta. There is no game fixture in the patch browse, no Play DOOM / Exit DOOM key in the pickers, no DOOM gamepad layout, and the doom verb answers that it is off. To switch it on for a night, run defaults write com.phantos.command phantos.doom.enabled -bool YES in Terminal and relaunch Command; run defaults delete com.phantos.command phantos.doom.enabled and relaunch to put it away again. A saved show that already holds a DOOM fixture keeps it — the fixture just has no profile until the switch is on.
